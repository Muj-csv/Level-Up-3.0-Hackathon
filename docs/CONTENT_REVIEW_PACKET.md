For: a PE teacher or cultural expert reviewing the Filipino street-game facts in
Taympers, per the PRD's F9.2 requirement ("Every fact is reviewed by a PE teacher or
cultural expert; keep the reviewer's name and date in the file").

## Pre-screen notes (not a review — read before you start)

An AI pass over this packet (25 Oct 2026) flagged two items worth your extra attention.
This is not a substitute for the PE teacher/cultural expert review above — nothing here
is signed off, and nothing in `content/fil.json` has changed because of it.

- **Q2 (Sipa as "national game until 2009")** — RA 9850 (2009) is solid: it declared
  Arnis the national martial art and sport. The "Sipa was the national game before
  that" half is widely repeated as trivia, but no prior law formally designating Sipa
  as a national sport could be confirmed here — it may be popular/traditional usage
  rather than a legal designation. Worth the reviewer double-checking the wording.
- **Q12 (Kalesa under the Plaza island)** — the kalesa is more closely associated with
  the heritage-street setting (Intramuros) than a fiesta plaza. Not factually wrong,
  just worth the reviewer's opinion on whether it fits better in a future "heritage
  street" island — a thematic-placement question for the team, not a correction to the
  fact itself.

Everything else in this packet reads as consistent with commonly published accounts of
each game to the depth an AI pass can check — which is exactly why the human review below
is still required before anything here can be marked reviewed.

## Part 2 — Minigame facts (8 games currently in the app)

For each game: the in-app name, other names it's known by, what you need to play it
for real ("Subukan sa labas"), the simplified in-app rules, and how it's really played
on the street ("Sa totoong kalye").

### Tumbang Preso
*Also known as:* presohan, tumba-lata, or tumba-patis in the Visayas
*To play for real:* one can, one slipper per player, 3+ players; draw a circle for the
can and a line to throw from.
*In-app rules:* throw the slipper at the can; the throwing line moves and you can only
hit it from the center white zone; 5 throws each, getting faster.
*How it's really played:* a "taya" guards the can inside the circle. Others throw their
slippers at the can from the line. If it falls, they run to grab their slippers while
the taya re-stands the can; whoever the taya tags becomes the new taya while they
retrieve a fallen slipper.
⬜ Correct  ⬜ Needs a fix  ⬜ Flag

### Patintero
*Also known as:* harang taga, or tubigan
*To play for real:* draw lines with chalk or water on a yard or court; split the group
into line-guards and runners.
*In-app rules:* cross every line to "Dulo" and back to "Bahay"; +2 per line going out,
+3 coming back, +5 for making it home; getting tagged sends you back to Bahay.
*How it's really played:* lines are drawn on the ground, sometimes with water (hence
"tubigan"). Two teams play: guards stand on the lines and can only move along them,
while the other team tries to cross to the far end and back. A tag swaps which team
guards and which team runs.
⬜ Correct  ⬜ Needs a fix  ⬜ Flag

### Sipa
*Also known as:* the national game of the Philippines until 2009
*To play for real:* make your own sipa — a washer or bottle cap with cut plastic or
paper; count how many kicks before it drops.
*In-app rules:* kick the sipa before it falls; tap when the ring glows; too early or
button-mashing briefly locks you out; it gets faster each round.
*How it's really played:* the sipa is a small weight (like lead or a washer) with
colorful paper or cloth attached. It's kicked upward using the inside of the foot or
knee, without letting it touch the ground. It was the national game until 2009, when
arnis was declared the national martial art and sport.
⬜ Correct  ⬜ Needs a fix  ⬜ Flag

### Palosebo
*Also known as:* from the Spanish "palo ensebado" — a greased pole
*To play for real:* played during a fiesta; ask a grandparent how they used to win it.
*In-app rules:* tap to climb a bamboo pole; hand turns green when you can tap again;
tapping too fast in a row makes you slip; reach the prize at the top within 12 seconds
for a speed bonus.
*How it's really played:* a fiesta game. A long bamboo pole is greased with oil or fat,
with a prize at the top like money or a flag. Participants race to climb it, which
takes strength, strategy, and patience to avoid slipping.
⬜ Correct  ⬜ Needs a fix  ⬜ Flag

### Luksong Tinik
*Also known as:* a game of jumping and courage
*To play for real:* two players sit facing each other on the grass; they stack feet,
then hands, to build the "tinik" (thorns); others jump over it; whoever snags it swaps
places with a seated player.
*In-app rules:* run toward the thorns and tap to jump when your foot is on the yellow
line; too early or too late and you're caught, ending your turn; the thorns get higher
and faster each level.
*How it's really played:* two players sit on the ground and stack their feet, then
their hands, to raise the "tinik." Others jump over without touching it. Whoever snags
it swaps places with one of the seated players.
⬜ Correct  ⬜ Needs a fix  ⬜ Flag

### Pukpok Palayok
*Also known as:* hampas-palayok or basag-palayok
*To play for real:* use an old box or paper bag instead of a clay pot so it's safe, and
fill it with candy; hang it from a rope, blindfold a player and spin them three times
before they swing; everyone watching stays away from the swinger.
*In-app rules:* look at how many steps away the pot is before the blindfold goes on;
count every step in your head, then tap to swing; a break is 10 points, a graze 6, close 3;
three tries; don't trust what the crowd shouts.
*How it's really played:* a game at fiestas and birthdays. A clay pot (palayok) with candy,
coins or toys is hung from a rope between two posts. The player is blindfolded, spun a few
times and given a stick. The crowd shouts directions, sometimes true, sometimes tricks.
When the pot breaks, everyone scrambles for the candy.
⬜ Correct  ⬜ Needs a fix  ⬜ Flag

### Taguan
*Also known as:* tagu-taguan
*To play for real:* 3 or more players and a safe yard; pick a base, the taya counts to 20
with eyes closed, and the others hide where grown-ups can still see them.
*In-app rules:* the taya walks the yard by itself and stops at every hiding spot; tap when
there's a sign of a kid (a slipper, hair, eyes, a shadow, a giggle, rustling or fingers);
cats, chickens and dogs are only animals; 10 points per kid found, a wrong tap is −2 and
locks you out of the next 2 spots.
*How it's really played:* the taya closes their eyes and counts while the others hide,
singing "Tagu-taguan, maliwanag ang buwan…". Then the taya looks for them; when one is
spotted, the taya races them back to the base. The first one caught becomes the new taya.
*Also for the reviewer (PRD N6):* is the taya's call/chant right for your region?
⬜ Correct  ⬜ Needs a fix  ⬜ Flag

### Piko
*Also known as:* piko-piko; hopscotch in English
*To play for real:* draw the court with chalk or charcoal on safe concrete or ground and
find a flat pamato (marker stone); even two players are enough.
*In-app rules:* a quick press is one foot in a single box, a press held a little longer is
two feet in a side-by-side pair; skip the box with the pamato, and if it is in a pair, step
with one foot into the other box; a wrong hop is "Patay!", back to the start and that
court's steps are lost; +2 per finished court, 20 seconds. Three courts take turns:
Klasiko (1·1·1·2·1·2), Eroplano (1·2·1·2·1) and Hagdan (1·1·2·1·1·2), with the pamato
in box 1 on the first court, box 2 on the second, and so on.
*How it's really played:* the court is drawn with chalk or charcoal. The player tosses the
pamato (a flat stone or a piece of broken pot) into the first box, hops on one foot in
single boxes and two feet in pairs, and skips the box with the pamato. On the way back
they pick it up without falling. A player is out ("patay") for stepping on a line,
falling, or landing in the wrong box.
*Also for the reviewer (PRD N6):* when the pamato is in one box of a pair, do kids in your
region step into the other box with one foot (as the game does), or hop over the whole row?
